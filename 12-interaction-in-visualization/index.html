Notes: Design principles are requirements and evaluation criteria; evaluation operationalizes them with a specified user, task, procedure and evidence. The five questions group the same seven principles, not a new taxonomy or validated numerical scale. The correspondence is many-to-many: these are useful starting points, not exclusive assignments. Meaningful control is evaluated through the relevance and cost of choices; Feedback through recognition of a response; Visible state through correct identification of active choices and scope; Clear mappings through prediction of an action's effect and scope; Consistency through whether that understanding transfers across related controls or views; Recovery through restoration of an intended state; Accessible interaction through operating and perceiving the interface with required input/access methods. Pages 25–28 show what to ask, what to observe and how to judge it. An expert inspection reveals potential problems; observed user task performance tests their practical impact. A working feature alone does not establish user understanding. Record actual evidence and limitations before proposing a change; never invent observations, numerical scores or usability results.
