Notes: 3 min. The perception–cognition loop describes the internal mechanism: visual evidence guides attention, while knowledge and goals guide what the viewer looks for and how it is interpreted. External cognition explains why a visualization helps that mechanism: the display keeps information and relationships available outside working memory, so each interpretation can guide another look, comparison, annotation, or revision. The visualization is therefore part of the reasoning process, not merely a picture produced after reasoning. Transition to Part 02 by distinguishing the two claims: Card explains why an external representation can expand cognition; Gestalt principles explain how the visual system extracts groups, paths, and figures from that representation. [Sources] - Stuart K. Card, Jock D. Mackinlay, and Ben Shneiderman, eds., Readings in Information Visualization: Using Vision to Think, 1999.
