Slide 10

Notes: 3 min. Preserve the Lecture 4 distinction: expressiveness concerns the meaning carried by an encoding, while effectiveness concerns the accuracy and efficiency of the required perceptual judgment. Separability asks whether viewers can attend to and interpret one channel without substantial interference from another. As in Lecture 4, separability is conditional and lies on a continuum: a strong size range can interfere with hue or shape. Capacity builds on Lecture 4 discriminability: attribute cardinality and value spacing must fit what the channel permits under actual viewing conditions. Distinguish too many color categories from size interfering with hue; the energy example has a reasonable nine-color palette, while thin bands weaken hue discrimination. There is no universal maximum number of colors. These checks interact but lead to different revisions.
