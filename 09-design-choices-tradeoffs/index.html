Slide 43

Notes: 3 min. Extend the principle of a shared visual grammar from standardized pictograms to multivariate glyphs. This is a conceptual generalization, not a claim of direct historical descent from Isotype. A glyph can be pictorial or abstract; its component features map attributes of a record, or can summarize a set of records. In the example, each of the six rays maps the same variable at the same angular position across glyphs. Ray lengths encode oriented ranks, not the original raw values. The left map shows individual French departments colored by region. The right map summarizes the median and quartiles for departments within each region. These are historical data, not current social indicators. Explain that glyphs permit variation in appearance under shared rules, unlike changing a scale independently for each item. Ask students to point to the same attribute on two glyphs.
